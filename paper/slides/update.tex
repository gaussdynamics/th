% --- 3. The design changed -----------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The Surrogate Design Changed}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  {\small The original plan combined three ideas. One was kept and two were dropped.}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  {\footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{4.2cm}L{1.8cm}L{7.4cm}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Part of the original plan} & \textbf{Status} & \textbf{Reason} \\
    \midrule
    Graph neural network: vehicles as nodes, couplers as edges
      & \textcolor{chartBlue}{\textbf{Kept}}
      & Handles any train length with one set of weights. \\[0.35em]
    Learn a correction to the physics equations
      & \textcolor{uniCrimson}{\textbf{Dropped}}
      & The training data come from those same equations, so the correction
        is exactly zero. \\[0.35em]
    Neural ODE: learn a derivative and integrate it with a solver
      & \textcolor{uniCrimson}{\textbf{Dropped}}
      & Stiff couplers would force 5 or more network calls per stored step,
        between states no data can check. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \begin{beamercolorbox}[wd=\textwidth,sep=7pt]{block body}
    \small\textbf{New design: a graph network simulator.} The network predicts one
    fixed 0.25\,s step. One network call replaces 50 evaluations of the physics
    equations, and more of the physics is built in than before, not less.
  \end{beamercolorbox}
\end{frame}

% --- 4. Why: the anchor measurement --------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Why: The Couplers Are Stiff at This Step Size}
  \vspace{0.1em}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}
        \begin{axis}[
            width=\linewidth, height=5.6cm,
            ybar, bar width=10pt, ymode=log, log origin=infty,
            ymin=0.01, ymax=3,
            symbolic x coords={none, euler, frozen, rk4},
            xtick={none, euler, frozen, rk4}, bar shift=0pt,
            xticklabels={{No anchor}, {Euler step}, {RK4, force\\frozen}, {RK4 step}},
            xticklabel style={font=\scriptsize, align=center},
            yticklabel style={font=\scriptsize},
            ylabel={\scriptsize Target Left to Learn (m/s)},
            ymajorgrids, grid style={chartGrid},
            axis x line*=bottom, axis y line*=left,
            enlarge x limits=0.18,
            nodes near coords, nodes near coords style={font=\tiny},
            point meta=explicit symbolic,
          ]
          \addplot[fill=chartRed, draw=none] coordinates {(none,0.0176) [0.018]};
          \addplot[fill=chartBlue, draw=none] coordinates
            {(euler,0.1108) [0.111] (frozen,0.1016) [0.102] (rk4,1.1869) [1.19]};
        \end{axis}
      \end{tikzpicture}
      \srcline{Median over 480 transitions of data/v2. Lower is an easier learning problem.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.43\textwidth}
      \small
      An \textbf{anchor} is a cheap physics estimate of the next state; the
      network would learn only what is left over.

      \vspace{0.5em}
      For velocity, \alert{every physics anchor leaves more to learn}, about
      6$\times$ more at best. One RK4 step of 0.25\,s diverges.

      \vspace{0.5em}
      The cause is stiffness: a coupler between two heavy cars oscillates with
      a period as short as 0.21\,s, and an explicit step of 0.25\,s is unstable
      for it ($\omega h = 7.45$ against a limit of about 2.8).

      \vspace{0.5em}
      For position the anchor helps: $\dot x = v$ exactly, so it is
      \textbf{113$\times$ better}.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

% --- 8. Result: velocity generalizes --------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Result: Velocity Holds Up on Longer Trains}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  {\small Trained on trains of 10 to 80 vehicles. Tested on trains of 121 to 150,
   which it never saw: 1.5 to 15 times longer.}

  \vspace{0.7em}
  \centering
  {\footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
    \toprule
     & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{Validation, 10--80 cars}} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\textbf{Unseen, 121--150 cars}} \\
    \cmidrule(r){2-3}\cmidrule(l){4-5}
    Velocity error & Model & vs.\ baseline & Model & vs.\ baseline \\
    \midrule
    One step, RMSE (m/s$^2$)         & 0.017 & 7.8$\times$ better & 0.020 & \textbf{7.4$\times$ better} \\
    After 200 steps, 50\,s (m/s)     & 0.066 & 27$\times$ better  & 0.118 & \textbf{11$\times$ better} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}

  \vspace{0.9em}
  \begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth}\small
    Baselines: predicting no change (one step) and holding the starting state
    (200 steps). Stable rollouts needed two training techniques together: noise
    added to the inputs, and training on the model's own earlier predictions.
    \alert{Doubling training time nearly halved the 200-step error}, so the
    model is not yet converged.
  \end{minipage}
\end{frame}

% --- 9. Result: shape does not -------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Result: Coupler Shape Does Not Hold Up on Longer Trains}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \vspace{0.3em}
      {\footnotesize
      \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
        \toprule
        Shape error, 200 steps & Model & vs.\ hold \\
        \midrule
        Validation, 10--80 cars   & 5.8\,mm  & 6.8$\times$ better \\
        Unseen, 121--150 cars     & 39\,mm   & \alert{1.2$\times$ better} \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}}

      \vspace{0.7em}
      {\footnotesize Coupler shape sets coupler force, and the safety limit
       in the control chapter is a limit on coupler force. This is the result
       that most affects the remaining work.}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
      {\small\textbf{Three explanations tested and ruled out}}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.35em}
        \footnotesize
        \item \textbf{Not too few message-passing rounds.} Error is even along
              the train, not larger far from the ends.
        \item \textbf{Not a steady drift.} Removing the drift made results
              worse on validation and no better on long trains.
        \item \textbf{Not caused by velocity error.} Giving the model perfect
              velocities removed only 9\% of the shape error.
      \end{itemize}
      \vspace{0.3em}
      {\footnotesize Per-step accuracy is similar on both sets. On long
       trains the errors \alert{reinforce instead of cancelling}: a stability
       problem, not an accuracy problem.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

% --- 10. Open problems ---------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Open Problems}
  \vspace{0.4em}
  \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.7em}
    \small
    \item \textbf{No corridors held out for the control demonstration.} With
          only four corridors, reserving one more leaves too little to train on.
    \item \textbf{The steep-grade test set is built on the rejected corridor},
          so it tests false grades rather than real mountain grades.
    \item \textbf{The national rail network is not yet split into routes.} It is
          a graph of 95,936 segments; turning it into corridors is the next
          piece of code. Everything above waits on it.
    \item \textbf{Coupler shape on long trains} (previous slide), which matters
          most for the control chapter.
  \end{enumerate}

  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth}\footnotesize\color{uniSlate}
    Planned fix for 1 and 2: extract corridors including real steep crossings
    such as Raton Pass, then rebuild with 50,000 to 100,000 scenarios, about
    2 to 4 hours on the GPU. Results so far will be re-run on the new data.
  \end{minipage}
\end{frame}

% --- 11. Decisions --------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Questions for the Committee}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1.0em}
    \small
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty scope.} Uncertainty is now used only as a safety
          margin on coupler force. Should Chapters 2 and 3 cut the
          probabilistic forecasting material to match?
    \item \textbf{Overspeed runs.} About 18\% of scenarios exceed the speed
          limit by more than 5\,m/s, because the simulator does not enforce
          it. Remove them from training, or keep them as hard cases?
    \item \textbf{Control demonstration scope.} Train the control model on
          all train lengths, which avoids the shape problem, and hold out
          corridors instead of train lengths?
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}
