# Surrogate Formulation — what `y_phys` is, and where the speedup comes from

_Written 2026-09-17. Companion to `PROJECT_DIRECTION.md` (which specifies
`yhat = y_phys + dy_GNN`) and `NEXT_STEPS.md` item 7. Closes an open design
question before model code is written. All measurements are on `data/v2` and
reproduce with::

    python scripts/measure_formulation.py data/v2

Percentiles are over 480 transitions from 40 `train` scenarios at ``--seed 0``._

---

## Divergence record

**The thesis diverges from its own locked design decision. This section is the record
of that, written to be quoted in Chapter 5 rather than paraphrased later.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Decided** | 2026-06, `PROJECT_DIRECTION.md`, "Locked decisions" |
| **Original decision** | Surrogate architecture: **GNN / Neural-ODE**. A message-passing GNN learns a correction to the known force balance, `yhat = y_phys + dy_GNN`, integrated as a Neural ODE. |
| **Revised** | 2026-09-17, before any model code was written |
| **New decision** | Surrogate architecture: **GNN graph network simulator**. A message-passing GNN predicts the mean acceleration of each vehicle over one fixed 0.25 s step. Position and actuator lags are imposed analytically, not learned. |
| **Cost of the change** | One design section. No code, no data, no retraining — nothing had been built on the old decision. |

### What changed, precisely

Three separate things were conflated under one phrase, and only one of them survives:

| Element | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **GNN / message passing** | **Kept, unchanged** | Nodes = vehicles, edges = couplers. Carries the variable-`N` universality claim structurally. Never in question. |
| **Physics-residual** (`yhat = y_phys + dy_GNN`) | **Dropped** | The residual is identically zero: `data/v2` was generated by `simulator2.torch_rhs`, which *is* `y_phys`. Separately, it is slower than the simulator it replaces. |
| **Neural ODE** (network as derivative, wrapped in a solver) | **Dropped, demoted to related work** | Coupler stiffness forces the solver to `h <= 0.05` s, so ~5 network calls per stored transition instead of 1, producing intermediate states no stored data can supervise. |

### Why this is not a retreat

The word "physics-residual" is being dropped, but the *amount* of imposed physics goes
**up**, not down. Under the original design the network predicted a correction to all four
state channels. Under the new one:

| Channel | Original | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `x` (position) | learned correction | **trapezoid (exact on bulk motion, 0.7 mm) + a small learned correction for coupler shape** |
| `v` (velocity) | learned correction | learned acceleration, integrated |
| `z_brk`, `z_trac` | learned correction | **exact analytic solution of `zdot = (u - z)/tau`** |

Two channels move fully from *fitted* to *imposed*, and a third (`x`) becomes mostly imposed:
the kinematic trapezoid carries the consist's bulk motion to 0.7 mm per step and the network
supplies only a ~29 mm shape correction. The network's output narrows from four channels per
vehicle to two scalars. That is a stronger inductive-bias claim than the design it replaces,
and unlike that design it is compatible with the speedup claim.

### How to present it

**Name it a graph network simulator** (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., "Learning to Simulate"),
with a fixed step matched to the corpus sampling interval. Do not call it a Neural ODE or a
physics-residual; it is neither, and the mismatch is exactly what an examiner will find.

State the caveat before anyone else does: the update
`v <- v + dt*a`, `x <- x + dt*v + dt^2/2*a` **is** a fixed integrator, in the velocity-Verlet
family. The claim is not that the integrator was eliminated — it is that an adaptive solver
calling the network repeatedly was replaced by a fixed one calling it once, and that the
network's job moved from *instantaneous* derivative to *mean* derivative across the step.

Keep the Neural ODE in Chapter 2 as related work and, if time allows, as one row of the
Chapter 6 baseline table. "We tried it, here is why it loses on this problem" is a stronger
sentence than silence.

## The question

`PROJECT_DIRECTION.md` fixes the surrogate as a physics-residual:

    yhat = y_phys + dy_GNN

and offers two readings of it in the same paragraph — "a message-passing GNN as
the learned part of `F_n`, integrated as a Neural ODE" **or** "a learned dt
step, graph network simulator style". These are different models with different
targets, different baselines and different speedup arguments. The choice cannot
be deferred: it determines what `y_true` is, and therefore what the data loader
emits.

## Formulation A — residual on the RHS (continuous-time Neural ODE)

    d/dt vec(H_dyn) = f_phys(H, E, U, R; t) + df_GNN(...)

**This is degenerate, for two independent reasons.**

**1. The target is identically zero.** `data/v2` was generated by integrating
`simulator2.torch_rhs`, which *is* `f_phys`. The correction the GNN is asked to
learn is `ydot_true - f_phys(y_true)`, and `ydot_true == f_phys(y_true)` by
construction. There is no residual — not a small one, an algebraically zero
one. The only nonzero content would be float32 storage rounding, at ~2e-6 m/s
against a signal of 0.

This follows from the surrogate and the data generator being the same model. A
physics-residual is the right design when `y_phys` is an *approximate* model of
data from somewhere else (real measurements, a finer simulation). Here it is the
exact generating process.

**2. There is no speedup even if it did learn something.** The claim in
`PROJECT_DIRECTION.md` is "speedup + fidelity-to-the-simulator". But a Neural
ODE of this form still evaluates `f_phys` at every solver stage — and then adds
a GNN evaluation on top. It is strictly slower than the simulator it replaces,
by exactly the cost of the network. The speedup story and the residual story are
in direct conflict under Formulation A.

**Formulation A should be dropped.**

## Formulation B — a learned large step (GNS)

    s_{k+1} = s_k + Step(s_k, U, R; dt = 0.25 s)

This is where the speedup actually lives, and it is worth quantifying.
`build_config.json` records `dt = 0.02` internal, `dt_s = 0.25` output.
Fixed-step RK4 at `dt = 0.02` is **12.5 steps = 50 RHS evaluations per stored
transition**. A surrogate that predicts one 0.25 s step replaces all 50. That is
the speedup ceiling, and it is a defensible number for Chapter 6 — it comes from
the integrator's own configuration, not from a hardware comparison.

The open question inside B is what to anchor the step on.

### Measured: the anchor makes the problem harder, not easier

480 transitions, 40 `train` scenarios of `data/v2`, sampled uniformly in time.
Statistic is `max |dv|` over the consist per 0.25 s transition — worst-vehicle,
which is the one a safety constraint cares about. Median / p90 / p99 in m/s.
Lower is a smaller learning target: it is the error a model that outputs **zero**
already achieves.

| anchor `y_phys` | median | p90 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **none** — learn `s_{k+1} - s_k` | **0.0176** | **0.0908** | **0.2231** |
| explicit Euler, `h = 0.25` | 0.1108 | 0.2192 | 0.4379 |
| RK4 single step, `h = 0.25` | 1.1869 | 7.5268 | 27.3700 |
| RK4 with `F_cpl` frozen over the step | 0.1016 | 0.1968 | 0.3911 |

Sanity check on the same transitions: RK4 at `dt = 0.01` reproduces the stored
next state to 0.0014 m/s median, confirming the RHS reimplementation is faithful
and the corpus is self-consistent.

**Every physics anchor is worse than no anchor at all**, by 5.8x at the median.
Anchoring on the instantaneous RHS makes the target *larger* and
higher-frequency than predicting the change directly.

### Why: the consist is stiff at 0.25 s

The coupler is a stiff spring between two large masses. Over 60 sampled
scenarios, the two-mass mode `w = sqrt(k (1/m_j + 1/m_{j+1}))`:

| branch | w median | w p99 | fastest period |
|---|---|---|---|
| buff | 17.4 rad/s | 29.8 rad/s | 0.211 s |
| draft | 15.1 rad/s | 25.8 rad/s | 0.243 s |

At `h = 0.25`, `w*h` reaches 7.45 — far outside RK4's stability limit of ~2.8,
which is exactly the divergence in the table above. An explicit step of 0.25 s
is not a slightly-inaccurate anchor; it is an unstable one. Stability returns
only at `h <= 0.05` (`w*h` p99 = 1.49).

And the couplers are not occasionally engaged — **they are outside the slack
deadband 91.1% of the time**. The stiff regime is the normal regime, not an edge
case.

Freezing `F_cpl` over the step removes the divergence (it breaks the
`delta -> F -> a -> delta` feedback loop) but does not help the target: 0.1016
vs 0.0176. The frozen force is simply a poor estimate of its own mean over
250 ms.

### But the anchor *is* valuable for position

Same 480 transitions, `max |dx|` over the consist, in metres:

| anchor | median | p90 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| none — learn `x_{k+1} - x_k` | 1.5818 | 6.7955 | 11.7254 |
| RK4 with `F_cpl` frozen | 0.0140 | 0.0367 | 0.0550 |

**113x better at the median.** The split is clean and it makes sense:
`dx/dt = v` exactly, so the position update is kinematics the network should
never be asked to learn. The stiffness lives entirely in `dv/dt`.

## Recommendation

**Formulation B, with the anchor chosen per channel rather than globally.** The
GNN predicts one quantity — the mean acceleration over the step — and everything
else is exact:

    abar_k, c_k = GNN(H, E, U, R)                        <- two scalars per vehicle
    v_{k+1} = v_k + dt * abar_k                          <- learned
    x_{k+1} = x_k + dt * (v_k + v_{k+1})/2 + c_k         <- trapezoid + learned correction
    z_{k+1} = analytic solution of zdot = (u - z)/tau    <- exact, verified to 4e-8 relative

**Corrected 2026-09-17 (same day), during the smoke build.** The line above originally read
`x_{k+1} = x_k + dt*v_k + dt^2/2 * abar_k  <- exact kinematics`, with no `c_k`. That was
wrong, and the correction is measured below.

Three consequences worth stating in Chapter 5:

1. **Two of four state channels leave the network entirely.** `z_brk` and
   `z_trac` obey `zdot = (u - z)/tau` with `u` piecewise-linear and known in
   advance; that is exactly integrable over the step. Giving them to the GNN
   would be asking it to learn a first-order lag it can be told. Verified
   against 25 scenarios: max error 3.6e-3 N on a 9.2e4 N scale, i.e. float32
   storage noise, and 100x better than an Euler step of the same lag.

### Correction: position is not free

The first draft of this note claimed `x` follows by exact kinematics, on the grounds that
`dx/dt = v`. The relation is exact; recovering it from the *endpoints* at 0.25 s is not.
Measured over 30 train scenarios, `max |dx|` error per step against the trapezoid
`x_{k+1} = x_k + h(v_k + v_{k+1})/2` computed from the **true** velocities:

| quantity | trapezoid error | no-change error | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| absolute `x` | 2.96e-2 m | 3.67e0 m | 124x |
| **relative `delta`** (what the coupler law reads) | **4.28e-2 m** | 5.47e-2 m | **1.3x** |
| bulk motion (consist centroid) | **7.03e-4 m** | 3.67e0 m | **5219x** |
| shape about the centroid | 2.92e-2 m | 8.75e-2 m | 3.0x |

The error does not cancel in the relative coordinate — it *grows*, because adjacent vehicles
oscillate against each other. At 4.28e-2 m it is **2.1x the coupler half-slack of 20 mm**, so a
scheme treating `x` as derived would put couplers on the wrong side of the deadband every step.

The reason is the same stiffness as everywhere else in this note: velocity completes close to a
full oscillation within one step, and the endpoints of an oscillation do not determine its
integral.

**But the split is clean and useful.** The trapezoid carries the *bulk* motion to 0.7 mm — 5219x
better than no-change — and fails only on the *shape*, which is exactly the coupler stretch the
GNN exists to model. So the kinematic relation is kept and the network supplies a small
correction `c_k` on top of it, with a ~29 mm scale and no bulk component. The imposed structure
still does almost all the work; what is learned is only the part that is genuinely unresolvable
from the stored grid.
2. **Call it a graph network simulator, not a physics-residual.** The imposed
   part is kinematic and actuator structure, not a force correction, so the old
   name would describe something the model no longer does. `a_phys_free` — the
   smooth, non-stiff terms only (Davis, grade, curvature, traction, brake) — may
   optionally anchor `abar`, since those are cheap and well-conditioned; the
   measurement above says the *coupler* term must not. Even with that anchor the
   model is a GNS, not a residual model: the anchor is a cheap feature, not the
   bulk of the prediction. See the divergence record at the top.
3. **The baseline table changes.** "Physics-only" as a baseline now means "one
   RK4 step at `h = 0.25`", which diverges. The honest baselines are:
   zero-output (0.0176 m/s median), constant-acceleration extrapolation,
   physics-residual MLP, and the demoted transformer.

## A negative result worth recording

Before the stiffness measurement I expected the 0.25 s grid to be **aliasing
peak coupler forces** — a 0.21 s fastest period sampled at 0.25 s is below
Nyquist, which would have put `data/v2` under Chapter 7's safety constraint.

It does not. Re-integrating six high-force scenarios at `dt = 0.002` and
comparing peak `|F_cpl|` against the same trace decimated to 0.25 s:

| | peak `|F|` at 2 ms | on the 0.25 s grid | missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| median of 6 | — | — | **0.4%** |
| worst | 586.0 kN | 574.9 kN | 3.7% |

The coupler is heavily damped — `zeta ~ 0.37` at representative values, so a
transient decays ~92% within one period — and the force is driven
quasi-statically by the slow bulk dynamics rather than ringing. **The corpus is
sound for the safety constraint, and `dt_s = 0.25` does not need revisiting on
these grounds.**

The distinction is easy to conflate: the *trajectory* is smooth and well-sampled
at 0.25 s; the *explicit integrator* is still unstable there. Stability depends
on `w*h`, not on how smooth the solution happens to be.

## The position correction must be learned per *edge*, not per node

_Added 2026-09-17 from the first smoke training run (`scripts/train_surrogate.py`,
N=42, 123 train scenarios). The model reached 2.5x the zero-output baseline on
acceleration and learned **nothing** on `dx_corr`: RMSE 0.86 mm against a target
std of 0.95 mm. That is not a training failure._

`state` is stored float32 and `x` runs to ~15 km, where the float32 step is
**0.977 mm**. The correction the network was asked to predict has a std of
**0.753 mm**. Signal-to-quantization ratio **0.77** — the target is mostly
rounding error, and a model that ignores it is behaving correctly.

The same physical quantity survives intact in the *relative* coordinate:

| target | scale of the coordinate | float32 step | target std | signal / quantization |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dx_corr` from `state.x` | ~15,000 m | 0.977 mm | 0.753 mm | **0.77** |
| `ddelta_corr` from `edge_dynamic` | ~66 mm | 0.0000075 mm | 0.877 mm | **117,713** |

The shape correction is real — 0.88 mm std, 1.77 mm at p99 — and fully recoverable.
It is simply not in `state.x`.

**Consequence for the model.** The correction head moves from the nodes to the
edges, and the state is reconstructed in the bulk/shape split the position
measurements already pointed to:

    abar_k        = GNN node head      -> v_{k+1} = v_k + h * abar
    ddelta_corr_k = GNN edge head      -> delta_{k+1} = delta_k + h/2 (ddot_k + ddot_k+1) + corr
    x_lead        = trapezoid          -> bulk motion, exact to 0.7 mm
    x_i           = x_lead - cumsum(delta_j + L0_j)
    z             = analytic lag

Absolute `x` is then needed only to look up grade, curvature and speed limit,
which vary on a metre scale — so its millimetre quantization is irrelevant there,
while the geometry the coupler law reads is carried at full precision.

### Outcome

Moving the correction head to the edges, and feeding the network the stored
`edge_dynamic` rather than features differenced out of `x`, on the same smoke
setup (N=42, 123 train / 16 val scenarios, 3000 steps, 24 s on the 4090):

| metric | model | zero-output | gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| velocity RMSE [m/s^2] | 0.0390 | 0.1314 | **3.4x** |
| velocity max\|dv\| median [m/s] | 0.00484 | 0.01673 | **3.5x** |
| shape RMSE [mm] | 0.219 | 1.003 | **4.6x** |
| shape max\|ddelta\| p90 [mm] | 0.952 | 4.206 | **4.4x** |
| shape max\|ddelta\| p99 [mm] | 3.747 | 23.544 | **6.3x** |

The shape head went from 0x to 4.6x. Velocity also improved, 2.5x to 3.5x,
because the edge *inputs* stopped carrying the ~1 mm position quantization
(which propagated into `F_cpl` at up to 17.8 kN through the coupler stiffness).

**The shape correction is sparse, and metric choice matters.** 64% of
transitions have every coupler under 50 um; the distribution of
`max |ddelta_corr|` runs p50 0.008 mm, p75 0.241 mm, p90 4.21 mm, p99 23.5 mm.
Almost all the signal is in slack run-in and run-out events. A median statistic
measures the quiet majority, where exact zero is unbeatable and beating it is
not the point -- the first version of this metric duly reported the model as
"9x worse than zero" while its RMSE was 4.6x better. Chapter 6 should report
RMSE plus tail percentiles, not a median.

**A sampling caveat for the real experiment.** The 16 val scenarios at N=42 have
a median mean-speed of 1.84 m/s (min 0.14, max 36.7): this split is dominated by
slow and near-stationary runs, consistent with the 22% reversal rate in
`DATASET_BUILD_REPORT.md`. Fine for a smoke test, not fine for a headline
number -- evaluation should be stratified by speed, or at least report the
distribution alongside.

**This retroactively justifies a schema decision.** `DATA_SCHEMA.md` stores
`edge_dynamic` "despite being derivable", flagged `edge_dynamic_is_derived`, as a
supervision target. It is not in fact recoverable from `state` at the precision
the surrogate needs: it is the corpus's only accurate record of consist shape. Had
it been dropped as redundant, `data/v2` could not train this model.

## First generalization result: unseen consist sizes

_Added 2026-09-17. Variable-`N` training, `scripts/train_surrogate.py data/v2
--steps 4000 --budget-gib 5 --ood`. 1,971 train scenarios (2.5M transitions,
N 10-80 in 70 buckets), 77 s on the 4090._

The size split is a genuine extrapolation test: **train covers N 10-80,
`ood_size` is N 121-150, with zero overlap** (pinned by a test, since the result
means nothing if that ever stops being true). The model is asked to simulate
consists 1.5-15x longer than anything it has seen.

| metric | val (N 10-80) | | ood_size (N 121-150) | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | model | gain | model | gain |
| velocity RMSE [m/s^2] | 0.0166 | 7.8x | 0.0198 | **7.4x** |
| velocity max\|dv\| median [m/s] | 0.00359 | 4.4x | 0.00810 | 3.3x |
| velocity max\|dv\| p99 [m/s] | 0.0396 | 7.6x | 0.0813 | 5.5x |
| shape RMSE [mm] | 0.216 | 5.2x | 0.265 | **4.7x** |
| shape max\|ddelta\| p90 [mm] | 0.984 | 5.0x | 2.145 | 6.6x |
| shape max\|ddelta\| p99 [mm] | 3.781 | 7.3x | 7.991 | 5.6x |

Gains are against the zero-output baseline computed on the same split, which is
itself harder on `ood_size` (0.1458 vs 0.1305 m/s^2): longer consists carry more
dynamics, so the comparison is not like-for-like in absolute terms and the
*ratio* is the honest number.

**Velocity RMSE degrades 19% (0.0166 to 0.0198) on trains up to 15x longer, and
the relative gain barely moves, 7.8x to 7.4x.** That is the universality claim
doing what `PROJECT_DIRECTION.md` said it would: nothing in the model knows how
many vehicles there are, because message passing applies one set of weights per
vehicle and per coupler however many there are.

Two secondary observations:

- **Variable-`N` training beat fixed-`N` on its own ground.** The N=42 smoke run
  reached 3.4x on velocity RMSE; training across all sizes reached 7.8x on a val
  set spanning N 10-80. More data (2.5M vs 155k transitions) and more diversity,
  same architecture and step count.
- **Bucketing, not padding.** `N` spans 10-150, so padding every batch to 150
  would waste most of the tensor on the small consists that dominate the corpus.
  Batches are drawn from a single size block instead: rectangular, no masks. The
  model never sees the difference.

Not yet done, and the next real risk: **rollout**. Everything above is one-step,
teacher-forced from stored state. Error compounding over a multi-step rollout is
where one-step simulators usually fail, and the standard fix (training-time noise
injection) changes how the sampler has to work.

## Rollout: the compounding failure, and the fix

_Added 2026-09-17. `surrogate/rollout.py`, 3000 steps, 4 GiB of train split.
Errors are max-over-consist, median over sampled start points, model run on its
own output from one true state._

**Without noise injection the model is worse than useless past ~20 steps.**
"Hold" is the trivial baseline of freezing the initial state and never updating
it:

| steps | seconds | max dv, no noise | max dv, noise | hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.25 | 0.0052 | 0.0044 | 0.0155 |
| 5 | 1.25 | 0.0517 | 0.0131 | 0.0669 |
| 20 | 5.00 | **0.2272** | 0.0317 | **0.2002** |
| 50 | 12.50 | 0.4447 | 0.0622 | 0.4523 |

At 20 steps the un-noised model (0.2272 m/s) is *worse than freezing the state*
(0.2002). At 50 steps it has simply converged to the baseline. Coupler stretch
is worse still: 40.4 mm at 20 steps against 7.6 mm for hold, a model five times
worse than doing nothing.

This is the standard distribution-shift failure. A one-step model only ever
sees states the simulator produced; its own small errors move it off that
distribution, where it predicts worse, and the error compounds.

**Noise injection fixes it, at 7x.** Training inputs are perturbed and the
target is recomputed against the *true* next state, so the label is the
correction that lands on the truth from a drifted start rather than the
correction from a clean one. At 20 steps velocity error falls 0.2272 to 0.0317
(7.2x), at 50 steps 0.4447 to 0.0622 (7.1x), and the model beats hold at every
horizon in both channels.

Noise magnitude, single-seed sweep at 3000 steps, velocity error by horizon:

| noise_v | 1 | 5 | 20 | 50 | val one-step RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.0052 | 0.0517 | 0.2272 | 0.4447 | 0.0207 |
| 0.005 | **0.0044** | **0.0131** | **0.0317** | **0.0622** | 0.0216 |
| 0.01 | 0.0053 | 0.0147 | 0.0372 | 0.0709 | 0.0222 |
| 0.02 | 0.0069 | 0.0206 | 0.0529 | 0.0909 | 0.0249 |
| 0.04 | 0.0104 | 0.0326 | 0.0861 | 0.1488 | 0.0352 |

Defaults are `noise_v = 0.005 m/s` and `noise_delta = 0.001 m`, which is roughly
the model's own one-step error in each channel -- the graph-network-simulator
prescription of perturbing by about what the model will drift by. There is a
real trade: more stretch noise helps long-horizon *shape* (9.5 mm at 50 steps
for `noise_delta = 0.001` with `noise_v = 0.01`, against 12.9 mm for the chosen
default) while costing velocity. The sweep is single-seed, so differences under
~20% should not be read as signal.

**One-step accuracy is paid for, not free.** Velocity RMSE goes 0.0207 to 0.0216
and shape RMSE 0.247 to 0.317 with noise on. That is the expected exchange:
slightly blunter one-step, dramatically more stable rolled out. Chapter 6 should
report both, because a one-step-only table would flatter the un-noised model
while hiding that it diverges.

**The harness is exact.** `test_oracle_rollout_reproduces_the_trajectory` feeds
the rollout the true targets instead of model output and checks it reproduces
the stored trajectory over 30 steps (< 1e-3 m/s, float32 accumulation). Without
that, rollout error would be partly harness error and there would be no way to
see it.

**Still open.** Noise is injected at a fixed std rather than scheduled, and
training is still single-step -- neither pushforward nor multi-step
backpropagation is implemented. Both are worth trying before the control
chapter, which needs stable rollouts of hundreds of steps, not fifty.

## Deeper rollout: pushforward, and where it stops working

_Added 2026-09-17. `--push` in `scripts/train_surrogate.py`, implemented in
`surrogate.rollout.training_batch`._

Gaussian noise guesses at what the model's error looks like. The **pushforward
trick** uses the real thing: the model takes `n` steps on its own output with no
gradient, and only the step after that is supervised. The perturbation is then
drawn from the model's own error distribution at its current level of training,
and tightens automatically as it improves. Memory does not grow with `n` --
gradients are not taken through the unrolled steps -- so the cost is one extra
forward pass per push.

Sweep at 3000 steps, velocity error (max over consist, median over starts):

| config | 10 | 50 | 100 | 200 | shape @200 [mm] | train time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| noise only | 0.0188 | 0.0581 | 0.0987 | 0.1614 | 12.3 | 57 s |
| push 1 + noise | 0.0185 | 0.0531 | 0.0914 | 0.1413 | 10.3 | 68 s |
| **push 3 + noise** | 0.0151 | 0.0424 | 0.0709 | 0.1219 | 10.4 | 90 s |
| push 8 + noise | 0.0169 | 0.0425 | 0.0644 | **0.0944** | 14.9 | 135 s |
| push 16 + noise | 0.0248 | 0.0501 | 0.0742 | 0.1129 | 9.8 | 224 s |
| push 1, **no** noise | 0.0236 | 0.0837 | 0.1311 | 0.2229 | 25.0 | 69 s |

Two things worth taking from that table:

- **Pushforward alone is worse than Gaussian noise alone** (0.2229 vs 0.1614 at
  200 steps). They are complementary, not substitutes. Plausibly because early
  in training the model's one-step error is small, so the pushforward supplies
  much less perturbation than the fixed noise does.
- **Gains flatten and the shape column is non-monotonic.** Velocity improves to
  about push 8; shape is best at 16, worst at 8, and mid at 3. That is not a
  physical story, it is single-seed variance, and it should not be read as one.

Default is `--push 3`: the cheapest point in the useful range, ~1.6x training
time for ~25% better velocity at 200 steps.

### Result at the default, 6000 steps

| horizon | val (N 10-80) | hold | ood_size (N 121-150) | hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 step | 0.0037 | 0.0158 | 0.0108 | 0.0279 |
| 50 steps (12.5 s) | 0.0275 | 0.4838 | 0.0608 | 0.5286 |
| 200 steps (50 s) | **0.0659** | 1.7807 | **0.1178** | 1.3066 |

Velocity at 200 steps is **27x better than holding the state on val, and 11x on
consists 1.5-15x longer than anything trained on**. Doubling training from 3000
to 6000 steps nearly halved the 200-step error (0.1219 to 0.0659), so this is
not near convergence.

### The limitation Chapter 7 needs to know about

**Coupler shape does not extrapolate over long rollouts.** On val it holds:
5.8 mm at 200 steps against 39.8 for hold, a 6.8x gain. On `ood_size` it very
nearly does not: **41.0 mm against 49.2 for hold, a gain of 1.2x**.

Velocity extrapolates to unseen consist lengths and shape does not. Two likely
reasons, both testable: a 150-car train has 149 couplers whose errors accumulate
along the chain, and message passing at 5 rounds reaches ~3-5 vehicles per step,
so coordinating a 150-car consist takes far more steps than a 40-car one.

This matters because the control chapter's safety constraint is on **coupler
force**, which is a function of shape, on **long consists**, over **long
horizons** -- precisely the corner where the surrogate is currently weakest. It
does not block Chapter 6, which reports velocity and one-step accuracy, but it
has to be either fixed or stated plainly before Chapter 7 leans on it. Worth
trying first: more message-passing rounds for large `N`, or a rollout-length
curriculum, or supervising `F_cpl` directly rather than only the stretch.

## Coupler shape on long consists: diagnosed, one fix tried and rejected

_Added 2026-09-17. `scripts/diagnose_shape.py`. Three seeds, 6000 steps each._

### What the error actually is

Signed per-coupler error over a 200-step rollout, which separates the three
failure modes that would each need a different fix:

| | val (N=38) | ood_size (N=150) |
|---|---|---|
| growth exponent (rms ~ n^p) | 0.37 | **0.66** |
| bias at 200 steps | +0.15 mm (2.8% of rms) | **+3.91 mm (26%)** |
| std at 200 steps | 5.47 mm | 14.73 mm |
| mean abs error by quarter along the consist | 1.28 / 1.25 / 1.39 / 1.90 | 11.4 / 8.8 / 10.8 / 6.8 |

Two hypotheses die here:

- **Not a message-passing range problem.** Error is uniform along the consist,
  slightly *lower* at the far end. Scaling `rounds` with `N` would have been
  wasted work.
- **Not an independent random walk.** n^0.66 is faster than the n^0.5 of
  independent per-step errors, so the errors are temporally correlated.

### The fix that was tried, and failed

`sum_j delta_j = (x_first - x_last) - sum L0`, so the sum of the corrections
over a consist is pinned by the end vehicles' motion, which the trapezoid
already carries. The true consist-mean has a std of **0.026 mm on train and
0.008 mm on ood_size** against a 1.04 mm per-coupler scale -- and it is
*tighter* on long trains, exactly where the drift appeared. Constraining the
edge head to a zero consist-mean therefore looked well-motivated.

It is not. Shape error at 200 steps, mm:

| seed | val, constrained | val, free | ood, constrained | ood, free |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 10.12 | 5.84 | 41.06 | 40.96 |
| 1 | 6.48 | 4.42 | 41.49 | 40.41 |
| 2 | 5.31 | 4.60 | 35.42 | 36.65 |
| **mean** | **7.30** | **4.95** | **39.32** | **39.34** |

**Consistently ~47% worse on val in all three seeds, and identical on
ood_size.** The constraint is available as `--zero-mean` and is off by default.

The mistake was reading "26% of the rms" as "26% of the problem". The rms is
`sqrt(3.91^2 + 14.73^2) = 15.24`; removing the bias entirely takes it to 14.73,
a 3% improvement, and the reported metric is max-over-consist where a 3.9 mm
bias against a 14.7 mm spread is invisible. **The dominant term is variance,
not bias**, and that was visible in the diagnostic before any code was written.
Why it actively *hurts* is less certain: centering couples every coupler's
gradient to every other one, which plausibly costs more than the freed drift
mode does.

### Where the evidence now points

Shape error is probably **driven by velocity error rather than independent of
it**. `delta` integrates `ddot = v_j - v_{j+1}`, so any spatially correlated
component of the velocity field's error accumulates directly into stretch. At
200 steps the ood velocity error is 0.128 m/s; a sustained correlated component
of under 1% of that accounts for the whole 41 mm, and it would also explain the
n^0.66 exponent. If that is right, the lever is long-consist velocity accuracy,
not a shape-specific mechanism -- and the cheapest test is whether shape error
tracks velocity error across checkpoints rather than varying independently.

### The velocity-driven hypothesis is wrong

_Tested 2026-09-17 with `coupled_rollout` in `scripts/diagnose_shape.py`, which
forces one channel to the truth at every step and measures the other. That is a
causal test; correlating errors across checkpoints would not have been._

| oracle | val N=38: shape @200 | ood_size N=150: shape @200 |
|---|---|---|
| none (real rollout) | 1.27 mm | 44.18 mm |
| **perfect velocity field** | 1.73 mm | **40.07 mm** |

**A perfect velocity field removes only 9% of the shape error on long consists,
and on val it makes shape *worse*.** Velocity error is not what drives shape
error; the ~40 mm residual is the correction head's own, on 149 couplers.

The reverse direction says the same thing: forcing the true stretch makes
*velocity* worse too, 0.0833 to 0.1388 m/s on ood_size.

### What that implies instead

The two channels' errors are **anti-correlated** -- the model has learned an
internally self-consistent effective dynamics in which its velocity error and
its stretch error partly cancel. Injecting ground truth into either channel
breaks that compensation and the other gets worse. That is worth knowing
generally: per-channel oracle tests on this model will *understate* how good the
joint state is, and any metric that scores one channel against a teacher-forced
other is measuring the wrong thing.

It also reframes the failure. Per-step correction error is only 17% worse on
ood_size, yet 200-step error is ~35x worse than val in the same comparison. So
this is not accuracy but **stability**: on val the rollout accumulates *less*
than a random walk (1.27 mm against the ~5.7 mm independent errors would give),
so errors self-correct; on ood_size it accumulates far *more* than a random walk
(44 mm against ~22 mm), so they reinforce. The learned dynamics is a stable
attractor inside the training size range and not outside it.

### Recommendation

Stop attacking this from the model side without a stability-specific idea; two
reasonable hypotheses have now been tested and rejected, and a third (more
message-passing rounds) was ruled out by the along-chain profile before being
tried.

The practical route is to separate the two claims, which want different models
anyway:

- **Chapter 6** keeps `ood_size` untouched and claims what is actually
  demonstrated -- **velocity** extrapolates to consists 1.5-15x longer than
  trained on (7.4x over baseline one-step, 11x over hold at 200 steps) -- and
  states the shape limitation plainly rather than burying it.
- **Chapter 7** trains its surrogate across the full size range, since the
  control demo needs accuracy on the consists it actually controls, not
  extrapolation to unseen ones. The held-out split that matters there is
  `control_eval` (corridors), which is empty for unrelated reasons and is the
  real blocker.

### The limitation as it stands

Across three seeds, ood_size shape error at 200 steps is **39.3 mm against 44-50
mm for holding the state**: a gain of roughly 1.2x, where val gets 7x. Velocity
extrapolates to unseen consist lengths; coupler shape does not, over long
horizons. Chapter 7's safety constraint is on coupler force, on long consists,
over long horizons, so this has to be fixed or stated plainly before that
chapter leans on it. It does not affect Chapter 6's velocity and one-step
results.
